## Custom Pool Cages

A pool cage is the centerpiece of outdoor living for most Florida homes. This aluminum-framed, screened structure surrounds your pool and deck, keeping bugs, leaves, and debris out while letting light and breezes flow through.

The payoff is a pool you can actually enjoy morning to night, with far less cleaning and a more comfortable, shaded environment. A well-built cage also adds a layer of safety and can boost the value of your home.

### Designed Around Your Home

Every pool cage should fit the architecture and lot it sits on. Roof style, height, frame color, and panel layout all shape how the enclosure looks and feels.

Upgrades like picture-window panels open up the view, while pet-resistant screen and no-see-um mesh tailor the cage to how your family lives. The result is a structure that feels custom, not generic.

## Pergolas and Shade Structures

Not every outdoor space needs a full enclosure. A pergola adds architectural style and welcome shade to patios, decks, and poolside lounges without closing them in.

Aluminum pergolas are especially well suited to Florida because they resist rust, rot, and insect damage while standing up to sun and storms. They define an outdoor room, cool the space beneath them, and give you a comfortable place to relax or entertain.

### Style That Matches Your Lifestyle

Pergolas can be designed to suit how you actually use your yard. Add hanging chairs and lounge seating for a relaxed retreat, or an outdoor kitchen and dining setup for entertaining.

Frame colors, beam sizing, and placement all let the pergola complement your home and frame your best views, whether that is the pool, the garden, or the water.

## Screen Enclosures and Rescreening

Beyond pool cages, screen enclosures protect lanais, patios, and entryways from insects, debris, and harsh sun. They extend your livable space and make the outdoors comfortable through more of the year.

Over time, screen wears out from sun and storms, which is where rescreening comes in. Replacing tired or damaged screen restores the look and function of an enclosure without the cost of a full rebuild.

### Keeping Enclosures Performing

A quality enclosure is a long-term investment, and routine care keeps it that way. Rinsing the screen and frame, clearing debris, and addressing small repairs early all extend its life.

When panels tear or sag after a storm, prompt rescreening keeps the structure looking sharp and doing its job, protecting your comfort and your home.

## Q&A: Outdoor Living in Florida

### What is the difference between a pool cage and a screen enclosure?

A pool cage is a type of screen enclosure built specifically around a pool and deck. Other screen enclosures cover lanais, patios, or entryways. Both use aluminum framing and screen to keep out bugs and debris while letting in light and air.

### Do I need a pergola if I already have a pool cage?

Not necessarily, but they serve different purposes. A pool cage encloses and protects the pool area, while a pergola adds focused shade and architectural style to a patio or lounge. Many homeowners use both to create distinct outdoor zones.

### How long do these structures last in Florida?

With quality aluminum framing and proper installation, the frames can last well over a decade. Screen typically needs replacement sooner depending on sun and storm exposure, which is why rescreening is a common service.

### Can you rescreen an existing enclosure instead of replacing it?

Yes. If the frame is structurally sound, rescreening restores the look and function of the enclosure at a fraction of the cost of a full rebuild.
